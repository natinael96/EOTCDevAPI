      Endpoints

      Everything is a GET unless marked. Years are Amete Mihret; dates are YYYY-MM-DD in either calendar. Full parameter tables, response shapes, and error contracts live in the documentation.

      Calendar

      GET/v1/todayToday, fully described. ?tz=Africa/Addis_Ababa
      GET/v1/date/{date}Describe any date: both calendars, weekday, fasting, feasts
      GET/v1/convert/{date}Convert between Ethiopian and Gregorian
      POST/v1/calendar/convert/batchConvert up to 366 non-contiguous dates in one call
      GET/v1/calendar/{year}/{month}One Ethiopian month, day by day
      GET/v1/calendar/seasonLiturgical season of a date, with a UI theme key
      GET/v1/calendar/geez-numeralArabic → Ge'ez numerals (2018 → ፳፻፲፰)
      GET/v1/calendar/icsiCalendar feed. Subscribe in Google, Apple, or Outlook

      Fasting & feasts

      GET/v1/fasting/{date}Is it a fasting day, and why: which fast, how long, day N of it
      GET/v1/fasts/{year}All seven canonical fasts with start and end in both calendars
      GET/v1/feasts/{year}Every feast of a year. ?type=movable|fixed

      Bahire Hasab, shown working

      Movable feasts come from ባሕረ ሐሳብ, “the sea of thought”: the traditional computation the Church has run for centuries. The API exposes every intermediate value, so you can check its work.

      ዓመተ ዓለምAmete Alem→ መደብMedeb→ ወንበርWenber→ አበቅቴ · መጥቅዕAbekte & Metqi→ መባጃ ሐመርMebaja Hamer→ ጾመ ነነዌNineveh, the linchpin→ every movable feast, a fixed Tewsak offset later

      The correctness argument: Fasika is computed two completely independent ways: the traditional chain above and the Alexandrian computus. The test suite asserts they agree for every year from 1800 to 2200 EC. Two methods with nothing in common but the answer, agreeing for four centuries, is not a coincidence.

      Two subtleties this API gets right

      Popular sources round these off. The arithmetic doesn't.

      ገና is not always January 7

      Ethiopian year 2016 began on Sept 12, 2023 (2015 was a leap year), so every date until the next Gregorian leap day shifted by one. Tahsas 29 fell on January 8, 2024, and Lalibela observed it there. Timket shifts identically. This API returns the true calendar arithmetic, not the approximation.

      The weekly fast is not absolute

      Wednesdays and Fridays are fasting days, except during the fifty days from Fasika to Pentecost (ሃምሳ), and except when a great feast of the Lord falls on one. Both exceptions are implemented and flagged in the response as fastFreeSeason and feastOverride.

      Where the data comes from

      Infrastructure you can trust has to say what it knows, what it inferred, and what it doesn't have yet.

      The calendar core is pure arithmetic: zero dependencies in both languages, verified against a shared fixture of 9,154 vectors. Vendor the modules directly if you'd rather not make a network call.

      The Gitsawe fixed cycle is transcribed from a printed edition (መጽሐፈ ግጻዌ ወመዝሙር ከነምልክቱ), preserving printed citations, scan pages, and transcription uncertainty. Seasonal and monthly cycles are imported from the open MIT gitsawe dataset with per-record provenance.

      Coverage is reported, never faked. Until the movable-cycle precedence rules are reviewed, /v1/gitsawe answers resolution: "fixed_candidate_only" and exposes known conflicts instead of pretending the fixed appointment always wins.

      Restricted text stays out. Bible verse bodies and unreviewed liturgical text are excluded from the MIT runtime until publication rights are recorded; references, structure, and metadata are served instead.

      Corrections are welcome. Feast dates, commemorations, and regional variations in fasting practice. Please open an issue with a source. A fixture diff is a behaviour change, and reviewing it is the point.
